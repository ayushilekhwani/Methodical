Jointing & termination

Bonding & link boxes

Cross-bonding or single-point bonding installed, SVLs fitted and tested, link box chambers set to level, earth continuity conductor terminated.

Discipline 02

Jointing & terminations

Almost every in-service HV cable failure begins at an accessory, not in the cable. Our jointers work in controlled enclosures to manufacturer procedure, and every joint is recorded — jointer, kit batch, ambient conditions, torque values, photographs.

Joint and termination types

  • Straight-through joints — like-for-like polymeric and paper systems
  • Transition joints — XLPE to PILC and XLPE to LPOF interfaces
  • Trifurcating and trouser joints — three-core to single-core transitions
  • Breach and dissimilar-size joints — mixed cross-section combinations
  • Insulated screen joints — for cross-bonded sheath systems
  • Outdoor sealing ends — porcelain and composite, with stress-cone assembly
  • GIS and transformer terminations — dry-type plug-in and fluid-filled
  • Indoor terminations — heat-shrink, cold-shrink and slip-on systems
Qualified on: Pfisterer · nkt · Prysmian · TE/Raychem · 3M · Nexans
Jointing in a controlled tent

Cleats, clamps and supports

Cleat selection is a short-circuit calculation, not a catalogue choice. We size to the circuit's prospective peak fault current and spacing, then install and torque to specification — an under-rated cleat becomes shrapnel on a through-fault.

  • Trefoil and flat-formation cleats, polymeric and aluminium
  • Cable ladder, tray, cleat rail and strut systems, galvanised or GRP
  • Vertical riser support and weight-bearing clamps
  • Tunnel wall brackets, cable racking and seismic restraint
  • Short-circuit withstand calculated per IEC 61914

Discipline 03

Link boxes, bonding
and earthing

On a long single-core circuit the metallic sheath becomes a conductor in its own right. Bonded at both ends it carries circulating current and derates the circuit; left floating it develops a standing voltage. Cross-bonding solves both — if it is installed and proven correctly.

Link box chamber

Arrangements we install

  • Both-ends bonding — simplest, but carries circulating sheath current
  • Single-point bonding — one earthed end, an earth continuity conductor, and an SVL set at the free end
  • Cross-bonding — sheaths transposed across three minor sections so induced voltages cancel
  • Link box chambers — set to finished level, drained, IP-rated, accessible for future testing
  • Earth electrode systems — rods, mats and continuity conductors sized to the fault duty

How we prove it

  • Sheath continuity — end-to-end resistance on every minor section
  • Cross-bonding verification — transposition proven link by link, not assumed from the drawing
  • Induced voltage measurement — recorded against calculated values under load
  • SVL operation test — verified to 15 kV, each unit individually
  • Sheath integrity — DC 10 kV for one minute per IEC 60229, before and after backfill
  • Earth resistance — fall-of-potential method with soil resistivity survey

Fusion splicing on site

Installation & splicing

  • Cable installation — direct burial, draw-in duct, micro-duct blowing and jetting, sub-duct installation, aerial ADSS and OPGW stringing on transmission towers
  • Duct works — rodding and roping, duct proving and integrity survey, CCTV crawler inspection, blockage clearance, micro-trenching
  • Fusion splicing — single-fibre, loose-tube and ribbon mass splicing, targeting ≤ 0.10 dB per splice
  • Closures — dome and in-line joint closures, splice tray build, closure sealing and pressure test, riser and aggregation enclosures
  • Termination — ODF and patch panel build, pigtail splicing, connectorisation, end-face inspection to IEC 61300-3-35
  • Restoration — emergency break repair, in-service hot cuts and planned cutovers under call-out

Testing & fault identification

Tier 1

Link certification

Insertion loss (attenuation) by light source and power meter, optical length, continuity and polarity — confirming the link meets the design optical budget. Attenuation measured per IEC 61280-4-1.

Tier 2

OTDR characterisation

Bi-directional OTDR on every fibre, because event loss varies with launch direction and single-direction shots carry measurement bias. Traces resolve and locate:

  • Splice events and individual splice loss
  • Connector events, loss and reflectance
  • Macro-bends and stress points
  • Breaks and high-loss faults, located to the metre
  • Attenuation behaviour along the whole route
